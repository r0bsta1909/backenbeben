User requested all three confirmed findings from the previous review be fixed.
Supersedes the original insert-only ellipse, contact-only emission pose and

1 / PADDED TABLE RIM
The old collider used only the inner insert ellipse. At x1.02/1.04/1.06,z.65,
falling drops missed the visible cushion and landed on the mat at y-5.166.
The renderer now caches world triangles from both PodiumTop and PodiumPadding.
An X/Z grid (cell.08 game units) and bounds reject irrelevant triangles. Finite
segments test the actual upward-facing surfaces, including curved padding.
Earliest table/floor contact wins. Solid bounce uses the surface normal; blood
splashes align to that normal, including deposits archived for later hits.
Approved visual meshes are read without modification; no new physics server
bodies or frame-order-dependent physics-world queries are needed.
New native regression independently ray-tests the actual padding mesh at six
rim locations. At x1.04,z.65: mesh y-1.293578, decal y-1.291044 (normal offset),
instead of a drop passing through to y-5.166. Opposite sides and both Z ends
also PASS. A dedicated sloped-rim splash checks decal orientation.

2 / BLOOD EMISSION FOLLOWS RECORDED HEAD MOTION
Previously every event used head pose at clip.contact, even if born later.
The host now linearly samples recorded pitch/yaw/roll and head position at each
event's exact birth, including fractional frames and endpoint clamping. Replay
frames' yaw retains precedence over the auxiliary rotation track, matching the
renderer. The existing spatial head blend remains applied at the source point.
Once detached, particles keep their independent trajectories; the correction
does not drag airborne drops along with subsequent head movement.
New tests verify every delayed drop against analytic interpolated translation,
and verify an ejected crown against interpolated replay yaw at its birth. The
constant-pose/legacy-track test and deterministic immutable metadata gates PASS.

3 / PRIORITY AND FAIR PARTICLE BUDGET
Previously blood from early regions could consume the96-event budget before
later bone fragments were emitted. The reviewed extreme case retained only two
mouth bone fragments and no nasal fragments.
All tooth/chip and bone events now precede decorative blood allocation. There
can be at most16 tooth/chip plus24 bone events, leaving56 slots even at maximum
structural damage. Blood sources share remaining slots round-robin, honoring
each source's requested count instead of starving regions at the end.
New regression:16 tooth losses +3 bone fragments in each of8 regions +7 blood
drops from each region =96 events. All important events are retained; budget,
unique IDs, finite JSON, no misses/fouls, no repeated tooth loss and local
bleeding/saturation behavior continue to PASS.

LIMITS
Bone shards and blood remain comic debris/particle effects. Triangle table
surfaces and plane floor contacts use small particle collision proxies; this is
not a whole-scene rigid-body solver or a volumetric fluid simulation. Moving
characters/clothing are not particle colliders. Single-machine tests do not
